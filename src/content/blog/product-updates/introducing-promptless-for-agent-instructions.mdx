Great skills and agent instructions can give an AI workforce superpowers. But as your codebase, tools, and workflows change, those instructions drift. Agents repeat mistakes, teams hand-hold them through the same dead ends, and yesterday's useful skill becomes today's source of confusion.

We call that **instruction debt**. Left alone, it turns into skill slop that makes your agents worse over time.

Today, we're announcing a new Promptless product that continuously improves the instructions behind your AI workforce. Promptless learns from agent session traces and turns recurring failures into better skills, subagents, hooks, and agent instructions—without those traces ever leaving your systems.

Think of Promptless as your Leverage Engineering team: the team responsible for making every engineer, operator, and go-to-market teammate more effective with AI.

## Why we built this

We launched our first product last year to automatically update customer-facing documentation. Then our customers started pulling us toward agent instructions.

Some teams literally adapted Promptless to update their internal agent instructions instead of public docs. At the same time, we saw the best technical writers solving agent performance problems that had stumped engineering teams. The same principles that make documentation useful to people—progressive disclosure, information architecture, precise language, and current source material—matter just as much when the reader is an agent.

Agent instructions also have one advantage that ordinary documentation does not: **the reader leaves traces**. Those traces show where an agent churned, hallucinated, leaked information, hit a broken tool, misunderstood an instruction, or needed a person to intervene. They turn vague complaints about agent quality into evidence you can act on.

We built an internal loop around that evidence and used it on our own engineering, go-to-market, and operations agents. The results convinced us this should be a product.

## How Promptless works

1. **Promptless organizes your agent instructions.** Your team's skills, subagents, hooks, and other instructions are versioned in a shared Instruction Hub.
2. **Each agent gets the right context.** The Instruction Hub is distributed as native plugins to your AI workforce and refreshed on agent startup. Each agent only receives the instructions it needs.
3. **The Trace Analyzer finds recurring failures.** Deployed inside your systems, it analyzes agent traces for churn, hallucinations, leaks, tool and MCP errors, interruptions, and repeated user corrections.
4. **The Governance Platform proposes improvements.** Findings become new, updated, or retired instructions. Changes pass an eval gate before they are merged into the Instruction Hub.

The result is a closed improvement loop: your agents do work, their traces reveal where the system fell short, and Promptless converts that evidence into safer, more effective instructions.

## What this looks like in practice

Dogfooding Promptless across our own team surfaced improvements we would not have prioritized—or even noticed—through manual review:

- **A better local development setup for everyone.** Promptless noticed that one engineer had built an unusually streamlined environment and turned that workflow into a reusable team skill.
- **No leaked sales-deck placeholders.** It detected repeated corrections to our sales-deck customization agent and improved the underlying skill before another draft reached a prospect.
- **Fewer authentication dead ends.** It found long-running Claude and Codex sessions degrading after universal auth tokens expired, then created a hook that prompts the user to sign in again.
- **Better incident investigations.** It saw agents overlooking our Datadog LLM observability tools, then improved our instructions and introduced a specialized subagent for the workflow.

These are small failures in isolation. Across every agent session and every teammate, they compound quickly. So do the improvements once the loop is running.
