## Overview

Passive listening has Promptless read the Slack and Microsoft Teams channels you choose and draft documentation updates from the conversations there, without anyone mentioning it. Use it on channels where questions and product decisions come up that your docs should reflect, such as a support channel shared with a customer. Promptless evaluates messages as they're posted and drafts a suggestion only when a conversation warrants one.

This page covers choosing channels, adding the triggers, and what to expect once listening is on. It also covers how to have Promptless act on a specific message.

<Aside type="tip">
  You can also ask Promptless in Slack or Microsoft Teams to set this up. For example, "@Promptless start passively listening to my `#support` channel. Draft updates from doc issues or gaps in those conversations." See [Ask Promptless to update your configuration](/docs/for-docs/get-the-most-out/ask-promptless-to-update-config).
</Aside>

## Before you start

- At least one [active doc collection](/docs/for-docs/connect/doc-locations/how-promptless-learns-your-docs). Without one, passive listening has nowhere to send suggestions and does nothing.
- An admin role in your Promptless organization, to edit the [Configuration page](https://app.gopromptless.ai/configuration).
- For Slack, the Promptless Slack app in your workspace. Promptless joins a public channel when you add it to a trigger. For a private channel, or a channel shared with another workspace through Slack Connect, [add Promptless to the channel in Slack](/docs/for-docs/reference/integrations/slack#add-promptless-to-private-channels) yourself.
- For Microsoft Teams, the Promptless Teams app installed with permission to read channel messages, and admin consent. Teams support is in beta. Email [help@gopromptless.ai](mailto\:help@gopromptless.ai) to turn it on for your organization, then see [Connect Microsoft Teams](/docs/for-docs/work-the-queue/reviewing-from-slack-and-teams#connect-microsoft-teams).

## Pick the right channels

Point passive listening at low-volume, high-signal channels. Good candidates include a Slack Connect channel shared with a customer, a dedicated `#docs-feedback` channel, or an internal channel where the team makes product decisions. Avoid a busy channel like `#general`, where Promptless evaluates many messages and few are worth documenting.

For example, a team points passive listening at the Slack Connect channel they share with a customer. Over a week, three people ask some version of "how do I rotate our API key?" Promptless notices the docs don't answer the recurring question and drafts a new FAQ entry. The team reviews and publishes it, and the next customer who asks finds the answer in the docs. Community and internal channels where the same question keeps coming up work the same way.

## Set up listening

1. On the [Configuration page](https://app.gopromptless.ai/configuration), open the **Triggers** tab.
2. Select **Add an automatic or scheduled trigger**, then select **A Slack channel** or **A Microsoft Teams channel**.
3. Under **Which channels should Promptless watch?** or **Which Teams channels should Promptless watch?**, choose the channels to monitor:
   - For Slack, choose channels from the list, or enter channel names without the `#` prefix.
   - For Teams, choose channels from the list, or paste a channel's conversation ID, which looks like `19:...@thread.tacv2`.
4. Select **Add trigger**.

Promptless tracks Slack channel renames, so renaming a monitored channel doesn't break the trigger. To stop monitoring a channel, edit or remove its trigger on the **Triggers** tab.

The Configuration page stores each trigger in `promptless.yaml`. To manage triggers as configuration, edit the YAML directly. This example has one `slack_listen` trigger and one `msteams_listen` trigger:

```yaml
triggers:
  support-channels:
    trigger_type: slack_listen
    match:
      - channels:
          - slack-connect-acme
          - docs-feedback
  teams-support:
    trigger_type: msteams_listen
    match:
      - channel_ids:
          - 19:abc123@thread.tacv2
```

For every match field, see the [Configuration Reference](/docs/for-docs/reference/configuration-reference#triggers).

## Know what to expect from passive listening

Promptless drafts a suggestion when a conversation shows a gap or error in your docs, or new information worth documenting. The result can be a correction to an existing page as well as a new one. Most conversations produce no change, and that's expected: Promptless stays quiet on ordinary chatter.

Once listening is on:

- Promptless doesn't post an acknowledgment in the channel. Its suggestions appear in your [review queue](/docs/for-docs/work-the-queue/assignment-and-routing#triage-the-review-queue) and in the notification channel set in your [policies](/docs/for-docs/reference/configuration-reference#policies).
- When a monitored thread keeps evolving, Promptless updates the suggestion it already opened for that thread, so you see one suggestion rather than several.
- Promptless processes the messages it reads to draft suggestions and doesn't retain them. See [Data handling](/docs/for-docs/security/data-handling-and-classification).

## Steer it in the moment

To have Promptless act on a specific message or thread right away, mention @Promptless in it. A mention runs immediately as a normal request, without the passive "is this worth documenting?" judgment.

Once Promptless is active in a thread, it keeps listening to replies, so you can refine a suggestion or add context without mentioning it again. To have it skip a reply, start the message with `aside` or `/aside`. An organization-level setting decides whether Promptless listens to all replies or only to mentions. To change it, see [Change the thread reply trigger mode](/docs/for-docs/connect/triggers/slack-messages#change-the-thread-reply-trigger-mode).
